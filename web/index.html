How it works

Three keys, and you never leave the window you were in.

  1. Super+Space

    Summon it

    The popup opens on top of your current app. Text you have highlighted, or just copied, can come along as context.

  2. EnterorSuper+Shift+Space

    Type it or say it

    Answers arrive as Markdown with highlighted code and a Copy button. Web search is on by default, so the full answer appears once it’s ready. Turn Web off with Ctrl+G to watch answers stream in.

  3. Esc

    Dismiss it

    The popup hides, but an answer in progress keeps going. Open it again and the chat is right where you left it.

Shell commands

It asks before it runs anything.

Turn on shell commands and the model can propose one. You see the exact command and where it will run, then decide.

  • Every command needs its own Allow once. Nothing is remembered as trusted.
  • Deny, Esc, or two minutes of silence all tell the model you declined.
  • File tools stay inside folders you pick. Directory listings and text reads can't climb out through symlinks.

Dictation

Or say it. Transcribed on your machine.

Press Super+Shift+Space and talk. Speech runs through a local model on your CPU, so audio never leaves the computer. Review the transcript, or send it straight away.

Bring context along

Show it what you're looking at.

Selected text

Highlight a passage in any app, then open Ntikuma. It arrives as a removable chip. Works on X11 and Wayland.

Clipboard

Attach what you copied with one click, or let it attach automatically every time the popup opens.

Screenshots

Pick a screen, window or area with the GNOME capture dialog. Ntikuma steps aside while you choose.

Live web search

On by default. The model can search and read pages, and cites its sources. Ctrl+G turns it off until you restart Ntikuma.

Your files

Ask what's in your Downloads folder. Read-only access, limited to the folders you add in Preferences.

Searchable history

Every chat is saved locally. Ctrl+H finds an old answer; follow-ups keep their screenshots and context.

Where your data goes

Your chats are stored on your machine. Each question goes to OpenRouter.

You bring your own OpenRouter key and choose the model. Claude Sonnet is the default, with GPT-5.1 as the fallback. Every question is sent with what the model needs to answer it, including the earlier messages in that chat.

On this computer

  • Microphone audio, transcribed by a local modelNever uploaded
  • Chat history and screenshotsSQLite
  • Your OpenRouter keyGNOME Keyring
  • Preferences~/.config

Sent to OpenRouter with each question

  • Your question, typed or transcribed
  • Selected text, clipboard and screenshots you attach
  • Earlier messages in the same chat, with their attachments
  • Files the model reads and output of commands you allow
  • Your local date and time
  • Web searches, which OpenRouter runs for the model

Install

Running in about a minute.

Ntikuma needs a GNOME session on Linux and an OpenRouter key. Voice models are separate downloads.

Debian or Ubuntu

amd64 .deb
$ sudo apt install ./ntikuma_0.1.0_amd64.deb

Includes the native speech helper. Check the download against SHA256SUMS.

From source

Go 1.26 · Node 24.15+
$ ./scripts/bootstrap.sh
$ wails3 build
$ ./scripts/install.sh

Installs to ~/.local/bin without sudo, starts in the tray at login, and offers to bind the GNOME shortcuts.
